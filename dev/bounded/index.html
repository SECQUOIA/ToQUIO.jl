Exact bounded-weight integer encoding

Select BoundedEncoding(; max_weight=2, levels=4) through the common interface. The global default remains NativeEncoding(). The strategy applies to originals and generated slacks and records both caps, the allocation limit and every actual affine map in schema version 2 metadata. Strategy identity uses name, construction and the recorded parameters together, plus the compiler revision for replay. Radix and bounded intentionally share the bounded-tail construction identifier; their names and caps distinguish the arms. Consumers must not key an arm on construction alone. It preserves the already selected native penalties without adding enforcement penalties. Strategy composition can use the interface's per-coordinate context.

Construction and guarantees

For integer endpoints L=ceil(l), U=floor(u) and width W=U-L, start at coverage C=0 and repeatedly append

\[w=\min(C+1,W-C,\mu),\qquad m=\min(\mathrm{levels}-1,\lfloor(W-C)/w\rfloor),\qquad C\leftarrow C+wm,\]

where mu=max_weight is a positive integer. The source coordinate is L+sum(w_j*d_j), with integer digits 0<=d_j<=m_j. Each weight is at most mu and each digit has at most levels values. Since w<=C_old+1, translated reachable intervals touch or overlap, so coverage stays contiguous. Since w<=W-C_old and both caps are valid, m>=1 and progress is positive. The endpoint never exceeds W and ends at W exactly. Every permitted digit assignment stays within the source interval, and every source integer has a preimage. Negative/shifted intervals retain their offset; fixed coordinates have no digits. Cap 1 is allowed, and a cap at least W gives the radix layout.

For levels 4, width 10 and cap 2, weights [1,2,1] with bounds [3,3,1] have endpoint 3+6+1=10. Nondivisible tails are explicit and can create duplicate representations. Cap 1, levels 2 produces unary bits; cap 1 with larger levels uses weight-one bounded integer digits. Duplicates change state multiplicities and sampling neighborhoods. Exact polynomial substitution does not imply equal sampling distributions or improved performance.

Levels 2 is an exact bounded-weight binary special case. Tests cross-check it against an independently evaluated construction: take the powers-of-two prefix while it fits the width/cap, then split the remaining width into repeated cap weights and one residual weight. The qudit strategy and shared canonical lift are tested separately from the reference; no vendor performance equivalence is claimed.

Invalid Boolean/noninteger/nonpositive weight caps, level caps below 2, empty integer intervals and nonfinite native bounds are rejected. Construction uses BigInt arithmetic; compilation checks exact representability in its coefficient type and rejects nonfinite transformed coefficients. max_digits=256 limits each coordinate before construction grows without bound. MaxEncodedVariables() independently limits the full target. Both are configurable software guards. Refused layouts report the width, caps, required digit count and configured limit before allocating digits. Raise max_digits, max_weight or levels as appropriate. Continuous variables are not discretized. Float64/BigFloat polynomial checks retain the chosen arithmetic precision described in the interface.

Weight caps and final coefficients

The encoding cap constrains B's integer weights in z=a+B*d. It does not constrain the final penalized quadratic or linear coefficients:

\[G_d=B^TGB,\qquad g_d=B^T(g+(G+G^T)a).\]

Products of weights, original source coefficients, offsets and unchanged native penalties can amplify these terms. A smaller cap can add variables and couplers. Report actual coefficients and check a concrete provider manifest separately; the weight cap does not certify device range, precision or embedding suitability.

Run the credential-free structural script from the repository environment:

include("benchmarks/bounded_structure.jl")

It compares a scalar quadratic x^2+3x+5, x in -2:8, and a model with two bounded integers and four inequality slacks. Its independently chosen feasible witness has energy 3 in every arm. The slack-heavy case uses the same native penalty vector [76,76,76,76] in all arms. It records dimensions, active variables, slack-owned auxiliaries, digit levels/weights, coupling structure and raw/canonical coefficient ranges. It invokes no optimizer backend or hardware.

Raw G is the stored matrix in z'G*z + g'z + c: its diagonal is the square coefficient and a unique off-diagonal coefficient is G_ij+G_ji. MOI quadratic diagonal terms use twice G_ii. Raw entry maxima describe storage; compare the unique canonical coefficients for polynomial coefficient ranges.

Canonical coefficients shift active coordinates by their lower endpoints, substitute fixed values into the constant, fold binary squares into linear terms, and use unique off-diagonal coefficients G_ij+G_ji. Density is 2*couplers/(N_active*(N_active-1)), zero for fewer than two active variables. The script also reports minimum nonzero magnitude, maximum magnitude and their ratio. The scalar bounded example's ratio is 4 versus radix's 24; this is a structural measurement on this model, not a solve-time or quality conclusion.

Provenance and replay

Karimi and Ronagh's primary preprint, Practical Integer-to-Binary Mapping for Quantum Annealers, motivates bounded coefficients in binary encoding. The inspected ToQUBO source at revision 69ac52592f4c47ed2168bf480c9947d1fcbbfc2c is MPL 2.0 (PSR, 2022). Its pinned residual-map coverage findings are recorded in the study protocol. This implementation independently derives that protocol's exact coverage construction and is covered by this repository's existing MPL 2.0 license. The reference provides design context, not a claim of reproducing its experiments or validating its implementation.

Use lift_native_state for the authoritative decreasing-weight, stable-tie canonical lift. Save the complete metadata and actual ordered digit state for replay; different valid preimages can produce different sampler trajectories. Parameter sweeps must record both caps and the compiler/source revision. The fixed campaign owns performance conclusions and tuning; hardware evidence has its separate approval and admission gates.

ToQUIO.BoundedEncoding — Type
BoundedEncoding(; max_weight = 2, levels = 4, max_digits = 256)

Exact bounded-weight integer encoding for original variables and generated slacks. max_weight must be a positive integer and levels an integer of at least two. The default optimizer remains NativeEncoding(); select this strategy explicitly. Parameters and actual maps are recorded in the encoding metadata.

For width W and coverage C, append w = min(C + 1, W - C, max_weight), m = min(levels - 1, (W - C) ÷ w), then increase C by w*m. This covers exactly the original integer interval without enforcement penalties. Fixed coordinates need no digit; negative/shifted offsets and nonunique representations are allowed. Levels two is a bounded-weight binary special case. Lower caps can increase the digit count and change sampling multiplicities and neighborhoods.

The cap bounds encoding weights, not the final quadratic/linear coefficients: source coefficients, offsets and unchanged native penalties can amplify them. It does not certify hardware admissibility. Construction uses BigInt arithmetic; integer map entries must be exactly representable in the coefficient type and nonfinite transformed coefficients are rejected. Floating products retain that type's precision and finite rounding is not detected. max_digits limits each coordinate's construction; MaxEncodedVariables() separately limits the complete target. Neither is a verified device capability.
